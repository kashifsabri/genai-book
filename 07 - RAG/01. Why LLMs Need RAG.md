

## Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) are powerful.

They can:

- Answer questions
- Write code
- Summarize documents
- Translate languages
- Generate stories

But they also have an important limitation.

They only know what they learned during training.

If the information was **not** present in their training data, they cannot reliably answer questions about it.

This is where **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** becomes useful.

---

# 1.1 The Problem with LLMs

Imagine you ask ChatGPT:

```text
What is the capital of Japan?
```

The model can answer:

```text
Tokyo
```

because this information was part of its training data.

Now imagine asking:

```text
What is my company's leave policy?
```

Can the model answer?

No.

Why?

Because it has never seen your company's private documents.

---

# 1.2 Another Example

Suppose your company has a PDF called:

```text
Employee Handbook.pdf
```

Inside the PDF it says:

```text
Employees are allowed 24 paid leave days every year.
```

Now you ask the LLM:

```text
How many paid leave days do employees get?
```

The model doesn't know.

The information exists in the PDF,

not inside the model.

---

# 1.3 Why Can't We Just Retrain the Model?

One solution might seem obvious.

> "Let's retrain the model with our company's documents."

Unfortunately,

this is not practical.

Retraining an LLM requires:

- Huge datasets
- Powerful GPUs
- A lot of money
- Days or weeks of training

Now imagine updating the model every time:

- A policy changes
- A new PDF is added
- A new product is launched

This would be impossible.

---

# 1.4 The Better Solution

Instead of teaching the model everything,

we can simply **give it the required information at the time of answering the question.**

Think of it like an open-book exam.

During the exam,

the student doesn't need to memorize every answer.

The student first opens the correct page,

reads it,

and then answers the question.

RAG works in the same way.

---

# 1.5 What is RAG?

RAG stands for:

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation**

Let's break it down.

**Retrieval**

Find the relevant information.

↓

**Augmented**

Add that information to the prompt.

↓

**Generation**

The LLM generates the final answer.

---

# 1.6 How RAG Works

Suppose the user asks:

```text
What is the company's leave policy?
```

The system performs these steps.

```text
User Question
      │
      ▼
Search Company Documents
      │
      ▼
Retrieve Relevant Text
      │
      ▼
Send Retrieved Text + Question to the LLM
      │
      ▼
Generate Final Answer
```

Notice something important.

The LLM is **not searching the documents by itself**.

Another system retrieves the relevant information first.

---

# 1.7 Example

Company Document

```text
Employees receive 24 paid leave days every year.
```

User asks:

```text
How many paid leave days do employees receive?
```

RAG retrieves this paragraph and sends it to the LLM.

Prompt sent to the LLM:

```text
Context:

Employees receive 24 paid leave days every year.

Question:

How many paid leave days do employees receive?
```

Now the LLM answers:

```text
Employees receive 24 paid leave days every year.
```

The answer comes from the document, not from the model's memory.

---

# 1.8 Benefits of RAG

RAG offers many advantages.

- Uses your own documents
- No need to retrain the model
- Easy to update
- More accurate answers
- Reduces hallucinations
- Works with PDFs, Word files, websites, and databases

---

# 1.9 Real-World Applications

RAG is used in many industries.

Examples include:

- Company knowledge bases
- Customer support chatbots
- HR assistants
- Legal document search
- Medical document search
- Financial reports
- Internal company wikis

Whenever an AI needs to answer questions using private data, RAG is often the preferred solution.

---

# 1.10 Real-World Example

Imagine visiting a library.

You ask the librarian:

```text
Do you have a book about Python?
```

The librarian doesn't memorize every book.

Instead, they:

- Search the library catalog.
- Find the correct book.
- Give it to you.

The librarian retrieves the information first.

Then you read it.

RAG works in exactly the same way.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Mistake 1

Thinking RAG trains the model.

It doesn't.

The model remains unchanged.

---

### Mistake 2

Thinking RAG stores knowledge inside the LLM.

It doesn't.

The knowledge stays in external documents.

---

### Mistake 3

Thinking RAG is only for PDFs.

RAG can retrieve information from:

- PDFs
- Word documents
- Websites
- Databases
- APIs
- Emails
- Internal company documents

---

# Key Takeaways

- LLMs cannot automatically access private or new information.
- Retraining a model for every data update is not practical.
- RAG retrieves relevant information before asking the LLM to generate an answer.
- The retrieved information is added to the prompt.
- RAG enables LLMs to answer questions using your own data.
