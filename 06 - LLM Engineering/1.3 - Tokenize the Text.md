#  Step 3 — Tokenize the Text

After cleaning the dataset, the text is still in human-readable form.

Example:

```text
Artificial Intelligence is transforming the world.
```

Humans can read this sentence.

Computers cannot.

A Transformer model only understands **numbers**, not words.

Therefore, the next step is to convert the text into **tokens**.

---

# 1.16 What is Tokenization?

Tokenization is the process of breaking text into smaller pieces called **tokens**.

Example:

```text
Artificial Intelligence is transforming the world.
```

↓

```text
["Artificial", "Intelligence", "is", "transforming", "the", "world", "."]
```

These tokens are much easier for the model to process.

Remember from the previous phase:

A token is **not always a complete word**.

Depending on the tokenizer, a token can be:

- A complete word
- Part of a word
- A punctuation mark
- A number
- A special symbol

---

# 1.17 Convert Tokens into Token IDs

The model still cannot understand tokens.

Each token is mapped to a unique number called a **Token ID**.

Example:

```text
["Artificial", "Intelligence", "is", "transforming"]
```

↓

```text
[7976, 4454, 2003, 17903]
```

These numbers are stored in the model's vocabulary.

Now the sentence has become numerical data that the model can process.

---

# 1.18 Why Do We Use Token IDs?

Computers perform mathematical operations on numbers.

They cannot calculate using words.

For example,

this is impossible:

```text
AI + Machine Learning
```

But this is possible:

```text
1045 + 2293
```

Once text becomes numbers, the Transformer can perform matrix multiplication, attention calculations, and many other mathematical operations.

---

# 1.19 Is Tokenization Done Only Once?

No.

During training, every document goes through tokenization.

Example:

```text
Book 1

↓

Tokenize
```

```text
Book 2

↓

Tokenize
```

```text
Research Paper

↓

Tokenize
```

```text
Website

↓

Tokenize
```

Every piece of text must be converted into Token IDs before training begins.

---

# 1.20 Real-World Example

Imagine you're moving to another country.

People there don't understand your language.

A translator converts your sentences into a language everyone understands.

The tokenizer plays a similar role.

It translates human-readable text into numbers that the Transformer can understand.

---

# Engineering Note

Modern LLMs always use the **same tokenizer** during both training and inference.

This is very important.

If a model is trained with one tokenizer and later used with a different tokenizer, the Token IDs will change.

As a result, the model may produce incorrect or poor-quality outputs.

For this reason, every pretrained model is distributed together with its tokenizer.
