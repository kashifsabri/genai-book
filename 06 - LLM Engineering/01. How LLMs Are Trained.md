

## Introduction

Have you ever wondered how models like ChatGPT, Llama, Gemini, or Claude become so intelligent?

How can they answer questions, write code, summarize articles, and explain complex topics?

The answer is simple.

They **learn from enormous amounts of text**.

However, they are **not** taught by humans one sentence at a time.

Instead, they learn by reading billions or even trillions of words and repeatedly solving one simple task:

> **Predict the next token.**

Every time the model makes a prediction, it measures how wrong it was, adjusts its internal weights, and tries again.

This process is repeated **billions of times** until the model becomes better and better at understanding and generating language.

In this chapter, we'll follow the complete journey of training an LLM—from raw text to a trained model.

We'll learn each step in the following sections.

---

# 1.2 What Does "Training" Mean?

Training means **teaching a model by showing it many examples**.

Imagine teaching a child.

You don't explain every grammar rule.

Instead, the child hears thousands of conversations and gradually learns the language.

LLMs learn in a similar way.

They repeatedly see text and learn patterns from it.

For example,

```text
The sun rises in the _____
```

The correct next token is:

```text
east
```

If the model predicts:

```text
west
```

it makes a mistake.

The training process adjusts the model so that next time it is more likely to predict:

```text
east
```

This simple idea is repeated billions of times.

---

# 1.3 What Does the Model Actually Learn?

The model does **not** memorize every sentence.

Instead, it learns patterns such as:

- Grammar
- Sentence structure
- Word relationships
- Facts found in the training data
- Programming syntax
- Mathematical patterns
- Writing styles

Over time, these patterns are stored inside the model's **weights**.

You can think of the weights as the model's knowledge.

During training, these weights are updated millions or billions of times.

---

# 1.4 Why Does Training Take So Long?

Modern LLMs are enormous.

For example, a model may contain:

- Billions of parameters
- Trillions of training tokens
- Thousands of GPUs

Every token requires mathematical calculations.

Repeating this process for trillions of tokens takes weeks or even months.

This is why training a foundation model is extremely expensive.

---

# Engineering Note

Training a modern LLM is not done on a single computer.

Companies use large GPU clusters where thousands of GPUs work together on different parts of the training process.
