# 3. Why Pretraining Isn't Enough

## Introduction

In the previous chapter, we learned that an LLM is first **pretrained** on a massive amount of text.

During pretraining, the model learns by predicting the next token.

After pretraining, the model becomes very knowledgeable.

It understands:

- Grammar
- Facts
- Programming languages
- Mathematics
- Writing styles

But there is a problem.

Although the model knows a lot, it still doesn't know **how to behave like a helpful AI assistant**.

Let's understand why.

---

# 3.1 What Does a Pretrained Model Actually Do?

Remember,

during pretraining the model learns only one task.

> Predict the next token.

Suppose the training sentence is:

```text
The capital of France is Paris.
```

The model learns to predict:

```text
Paris
```

Now consider another sentence.

```text
The cat sat on the
```

The model learns to predict:

```text
mat
```

This is how it learns language.

Notice something important.

Nobody teaches the model:

- How to answer politely
- How to follow instructions
- How to chat with users

It only learns to predict the next token.

---

# 3.2 A Pretrained Model Doesn't Understand Instructions

Suppose you ask:

```text
Translate this sentence into French.

I love AI.
```

A pretrained model has never been specifically trained to follow instructions.

Instead of translating,

it may continue writing something like:

```text
Translate this sentence into French.

I love AI.

Artificial Intelligence is changing the world...
```

Why?

Because it is simply predicting the next most likely token.

It is not trying to complete your task.

---

# 3.3 Another Example

You ask:

```text
Write a short poem about the moon.
```

A pretrained model might produce something strange like:

```text
Write a short poem about the moon.

Question 2

Answer:

The moon...
```

This happens because similar text existed in its training data.

The model is completing patterns.

It is not behaving like an assistant.

---

# 3.4 It Doesn't Know What the User Wants

Imagine someone says:

```text
Summarize this article.
```

A pretrained model doesn't truly understand that your goal is to create a summary.

It only sees another sequence of tokens.

Without additional training,

it may:

- Continue the article
- Repeat parts of it
- Produce unrelated text

Instead of generating a proper summary.

---

# 3.5 Why ChatGPT Feels Different

Now think about ChatGPT.

If you ask:

```text
Explain Machine Learning in simple words.
```

ChatGPT usually:

- Understands your instruction.
- Gives a simple explanation.
- Organizes the answer.
- Uses an appropriate tone.

This behavior did **not** come from pretraining alone.

It comes from additional training after pretraining.

---

# 3.6 Real-World Example

Imagine a medical student.

After five years of study,

the student knows a lot about medicine.

But on the first day at the hospital,

they may not know how to:

- Talk to patients
- Explain diseases simply
- Handle real conversations

An experienced doctor teaches these practical skills.

The same thing happens with LLMs.

Pretraining gives knowledge.

Additional training teaches behavior.

---

# 3.7 The Next Step

After pretraining,

AI engineers collect thousands of examples like this.

```text
Instruction:

Explain gravity to a 10-year-old.
```

```text
Ideal Answer:

Gravity is the force that pulls objects toward the Earth...
```

The model is trained to produce the ideal answer instead of simply predicting random continuations.

This process is called:

**Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**

We'll study it in the next chapter.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

### Mistake 1

Thinking pretraining creates ChatGPT.

It doesn't.

Pretraining creates a foundation model.

---

### Mistake 2

Thinking a pretrained model always follows instructions.

It doesn't.

Instruction-following is learned later.

---

### Mistake 3

Thinking pretraining and chatting are the same thing.

They are different.

Pretraining teaches language.

Fine-tuning teaches how to interact with users.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Pretraining teaches the model language by predicting the next token.
- A pretrained model does not automatically become a chatbot.
- It does not naturally understand instructions or user intent.
- Additional training is needed to make the model helpful and conversational.
- The first step after pretraining is **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**.
